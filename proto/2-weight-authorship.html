Designing inside constraints you did not choose is a different skill.

Three were already true before I arrived. Each produced a decision I can defend, including what it cost.

  1. Users arrived already fluent in someone else's interface. An inherited mental model
  2. Two features had become one idea in everyone's head but the IA. Two concepts, one product
  3. No grade, no credit, no manager. Attendance was the whole problem. Students with no reason to return
Context
Prior fluency is an asset until you contradict it. Students landed expecting a thread list, streaming tokens, and a message they could edit and resend.
Rejected
A distinct interaction paradigm — a document canvas with the model as a margin collaborator. It demoed well.
Cost
The product is derivative in a screenshot, which is the format procurement sees it in first.
